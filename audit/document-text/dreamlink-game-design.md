**DreamLink: The Fractured Timeline**

### Core Premise

Two players wake up as different versions of the same person, split between two dimensions:

* Player 1 – Reality (R): The physical world, bound by logic and time.

* Player 2 – Dream (D): The subconscious realm, where imagination rewrites the rules.

Their shared goal: merge the broken timeline before they fall asleep again.  
 If they fail, their soul stays divided — one lost forever in the Dream.

---

## Gameplay Concept (Co-op Core)

\-Both players must work together across their separate realms.  
\-Worlds are interconnected — an action in one dimension affects the other.  
\-They communicate to solve puzzles, open doors, and synchronize their actions.

Example:

\-Player 1 presses a real-world switch → creates a floating bridge in Player 2’s world.  
\-Player 2 manipulates dream gravity → moves an object in Player R’s world.

The link between worlds strengthens as they cooperate — and weakens if they desync.

## **LEVEL 1 – “The Awakening”**

###  Setting

Both players wake up in mirrored rooms —

\-Player 1: Reality (normal room)  
\-Player 2: Dream (slightly distorted version of the same room)

Each has:

\-A closed door glowing faintly.  
\-A floor switch nearby.  
\-A few random objects like a radio, a cracked clock, and a chair — for visual confusion.

---

##  Puzzle Setup

When one player steps on the switch:

\-The door’s border glows halfway — like it’s charging.  
\-But it doesn’t open.  
 When the other player steps on their switch at the same time:  
\-Both doors fully glow and open together with a satisfying “click” \+ visual pulse.

---

 Simple In-World Hint (Riddle Style)

A message is scribbled faintly on the wall near the door

>  “One light can’t wake the door...  
>  Two lights, and time will move once more.”

or

>  “Half the glow means half the key.  
>  Move as one, and you’ll be free.”

Both are short, readable, and immediately hint that doing something together will unlock the door — but still make the player *feel smart* for figuring it out.

---

 Aha Moment Design

\-Player A steps → door half-glows.  
\-Player B steps → their door half-glows.  
\-They realize, “Oh wait… maybe together\!”  
\-They both step — doors flash, open at the same time, satisfying chime plays.

Subtle reward moments:

\-Small screen shake \+ glowing light linking both doors.

*\-*Transition to next level.

---

## Extra Objects (for confusion / world depth)

\-Clock (12:12) – ticks when one switch is pressed, stops otherwise.  
\-Broken Radio – static noise, no help.  
\-Mirror – reflects faint movement of the other world for aesthetic effect.

These keep the room from feeling empty but don’t distract from the main puzzle.

## **LEVEL 2 – “The Mark of Echoes”**

###  Setting

Both players (Reality & Dream versions of Evan) awaken inside an ancient, half-collapsed chamber.  
 The air hums faintly, like the world itself remembers something forgotten.  
 On the wall — the same carved mark from his dreams, circular and ancient, but dull and lifeless.

This is the first real clue about what’s happening to him.

---

 Main Goal (Narrative \+ Gameplay Unified)

> To awaken the carved mark, revealing the first piece of truth about why Evan’s consciousness is split between two realms.

When the mark is reactivated, it projects a symbolic vision or message —  
 showing a fragment of the origin of the fracture between the Dream and Reality.

---

## Gameplay Flow

### Step 1 – The Silent Mark

\-Both players find the same dull carving.  
\-Touching it only flickers faint light — not enough to awaken it.  
\-A faint riddle glows briefly beneath:

 “When thought and form stand aligned,  
 the sleeping mark shall speak of time.”

 This hints that shapes (form) and communication (thought) must align across both worlds.

---

### Step 2 – The Block Alignment Puzzle

\-Reality Player: Finds 3–5 stone blocks — can move them.  
\-Dream Player: Sees the correct arrangement pattern of those same shapes glowing faintly on the wall.

They must communicate to align them correctly.  
 Once done, blocks float slightly, glowing:

\-🟩 Green \= correctly placed.  
\-🟥 Red \= wrong.

When all are green — the carved mark fully awakens.

---

### Step 3 – The Revelation

\-The glowing mark activates simultaneously in both worlds.  
\-A beam of light emerges, forming a holographic projection in the Dream realm,  
 and etched words in the Reality realm.

> Dream Vision (Player 2 sees visuals):  
>  A glimpse of a tower split in two, its core pulsing with time energy —  
>  the moment Evan’s experiment failed or the moment he tried to fix time itself.

> Reality Message (Player 1 reads):  
>  “Subject E-07. Temporal split occurred at 03:17.  
>  Both halves remain alive. Containment failed.”

Evan realizes he wasn’t “chosen” by destiny —  
 he was part of an experiment to connect human consciousness between dream and reality — and it went terribly wrong.

---

###  End of Level Moment

As the vision fades, both marks pulse once more.  
 The chamber shakes — walls shift, revealing a pathway of light leading deeper into the unknown.

> *Voice (calm, distant):*  
>  “You were meant to bridge the worlds… but not like this.”

Both players step forward into the glowing corridor — leading to Level 3\.

---

##  What the Player Achieves

\-Gameplay: They solved a more complex co-op puzzle (observe \+ execute).  
\-Narrative: They unlocked the first truth — Evan wasn’t “chosen,” he was created to be a link.  
\-Emotional tone: Curiosity replaces confusion — the player now has a *goal with purpose*:

 a 2D mobile platformer. Both players will be in identical-looking sections of   

Shared Elements (for both Reality and Dream players):

The Cart: A sturdy, cartoonish mine cart with space for a character. It will be centrally placed on the screen, moving horizontally.

The Cave Environment: Dark, rocky walls, perhaps with glowing crystals or moss. The "up and down" tracks will be visible.

Track: A metal rail system, with one set on the floor and another mirrored on the ceiling.

Speed: A consistent, moderate speed for the cart.  
Two Buttons (Up/Down): Both players will have distinct "Up" and "Down" buttons to control their cart's position on the track. If a button is pressed, the cart will seamlessly transition to the top or bottom track.

Dream World Track Weakness (Visualized): In the Dream player's view, sections of the track will show clear signs of weakness – perhaps glowing cracks, ethereal gaps, or unstable-looking segments. These are the areas where the cart cannot safely travel on that specific track (top or bottom).

Reality World Action (Jump/Duck): In the Reality player's view, instead of just track switching, pressing "Up" will make the cart perform a "jump" (to clear a bottom-track gap), and pressing "Down" will make the cart "duck" (to avoid a top-track obstacle). The visual cues for these actions will be clear.

Simultaneous Play: The UI for both players will be clearly visible, emphasizing the need for coordinated input.
